## Bullets in order
1. The model’s performance may vary based on the quality of deepfake videos. It is likely to perform well on high-resolution, high-quality deepfakes but may struggle with low-resolution or noisy videos, where subtle manipulation artifacts can be difficult to detect.
2. This study focuses exclusively on video-based deepfake detection and does not consider audio manipulations. As a result, the model may not be effective in detecting multimodal deepfakes that combine both video and audio manipulations.
3. Video-level decision. In the benchmark evaluation, each video is judged from a fixed number of short face clips sampled at a reduced frame rate, and the clip scores are averaged into one video-level score. A manipulation that affects only a short part of a longer video, or an artifact that appears only between consecutive frames at the original frame rate, may therefore have a weaker effect on the final score, and short clips, low-frame-rate footage, or videos with little facial motion give the temporal stream less evidence.
4. Performance and processing time may be affected by the input video’s duration, frame rate, and file size, since longer/higher-FPS videos require more frames to be analyzed. Detection may also degrade under heavy compression and other quality losses (e.g., blur/noise/low-light), which can mask the visual artifacts the model relies on.
5. The study’s findings are based on public benchmark datasets; performance may vary when applied to real-world videos with unseen manipulation methods, diverse capture devices, or post-processing pipelines.
6. Manipulation types. From FaceForensics++, only the DeepFakes subset is used; the other manipulation subsets, such as Face2Face, FaceSwap, and NeuralTextures, are not. All three benchmarks therefore contain face swaps only, and because they were released in 2019 and 2020, the results do not cover expression reenactment, lip-sync manipulation, fully synthetic faces, diffusion-based video generation, or face-swap tools released after 2020.
7. Population coverage. The benchmark datasets were not collected to represent Filipino subjects or Philippine media, and performance is not measured separately by age, sex, or skin tone. Equal performance across demographic groups is therefore not established.
8. Shared footage. DeeperForensics-1.0 creates its manipulated videos by swapping new faces into FaceForensics++ videos, and its real class in this study consists of those FaceForensics++ originals, so transfer between these two datasets is not fully independent; subsets without shared footage are analyzed but are small (20 to 60 videos for these directions). Transfer from FaceForensics++ C23 to C40 tests the same videos under stronger compression rather than a new dataset. In addition, the face-preprocessing rules were developed on training videos, some of which share footage with held-out videos, and frames from some held-out videos were viewed, without labels or scores, during face-quality review.
9. The model’s performance may be affected by face detection and alignment reliability; failures under occlusion, extreme head pose, or poor lighting may reduce detection accuracy.
10. Face-centric evidence focus. The SwinFusion++ pipeline relies on face detection/alignment and therefore prioritizes face-centered manipulations. Videos where manipulation is primarily non-facial (e.g., body/scene edits) or where faces are not consistently detectable may not be well-served by the system’s evidence extraction strategy.
11. Benchmark ceiling and transfer. When trained and tested on the same benchmark, the three models compared in this study all score close to the maximum, so differences between them within a single benchmark are small and hard to detect. Every model, SwinFusion++ included, performs less well on datasets it was not trained on. Differences between the models are therefore more informative under cross-dataset transfer, and any transfer advantage is relative to the compared encoders; detection on unseen data remains an open problem.
12. Statistical precision. The test sets contain 280 videos for each FaceForensics++ quality level, 978 for Celeb-DF v2, and 402 for DeeperForensics-1.0, with related videos kept in the same partition. All manipulated Celeb-DF v2 test videos come from nine identities, so its confidence intervals are approximate, and most of its test videos are manipulated, so its accuracy is read together with ROC-AUC. SwinFusion++, Swin-B, and Video Swin-B are each trained with three random seeds and evaluated as three-seed ensembles, which show whether results are stable across training runs but do not measure seed-to-seed variation precisely. Small differences between models may therefore go undetected.
13. Published comparisons. Established baselines, including the CNN-LSTM models, CAST, and TSFF-Net, are not retrained; their results are taken from published papers, which use their own data splits, preprocessing, and evaluation units, so these comparisons place SwinFusion++ in context rather than serving as controlled head-to-head tests.
14. Training recipe and tuning. In the principal comparison, SwinFusion++, Swin-B, and Video Swin-B are trained with one shared fixed recipe, so none receives tuning the others do not, although the shared recipe may not be the best setting for each model. Bayesian hyperparameter optimization is applied to SwinFusion++ only, so the optimized model is reported separately as a configured system and is not used to judge the architecture.
15. Model size and cost. SwinFusion++ runs both backbones, so it has about 181 million parameters, against about 87 million for Swin-B and 88 million for Video Swin-B, and needs about twice the training memory of one backbone; the fusion module itself adds only about 6.5 million parameters. Inference latency on the deployment server is not benchmarked, and the model is not compressed (for example, by pruning, distillation, or quantization). Because two encoders are combined, part of an advantage over a single encoder may come from the combination rather than from the fusion module; Chapter 4 examines this under RQ4.
16. Decision thresholds and confidence values. Each model’s decision threshold is chosen on the validation data of its training dataset and kept unchanged when the model is tested on other datasets. On a dataset with a different distribution, a fixed threshold can make a detector too cautious or too permissive, so threshold-based measures (accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score) under transfer are read together with ROC-AUC, which does not depend on a single threshold. For the same reason, the platform’s verdict and confidence value are most reliable for videos similar to the training data.
17. Binary decision support, not forensic proof. The platform produces a probabilistic classification output (Real/Fake with confidence and metadata). This is decision support for screening and verification workflows, but it does not, by itself, constitute definitive proof of authenticity for legal or evidentiary contexts.
18. Evidence maps. The evidence maps shown by the platform highlight the facial regions that most influenced a result. They are not validated as faithful explanations of the model’s decision, and they do not identify which frames or regions were manipulated or which manipulation method was used.
19. Platform and benchmark results. The detection results in Chapter 4 come from the offline benchmark evaluation, which scores seven fixed clips per video. The platform instead analyzes the whole video for its verdict, so its outputs can differ from the benchmark results, and its own detection accuracy is not measured. The detector recommendations and the Auto option’s selection rules describe the typical content of each training dataset and are not evaluated. The user and expert evaluation assesses the platform’s quality in use, not its detection accuracy.
20. Prototype deployment constraints. Inference time and throughput are dependent on the deployment environment (server hardware, concurrency, and upload bandwidth). The platform is evaluated as a functional research prototype and may require additional engineering for high-traffic production environments (e.g., load balancing, rate limiting, hardened storage policies, and formal penetration testing).
21. The deployed web-based prototype accepts one video per submission only, with a length of 10 seconds to 2 minutes, a resolution of 360p to 1080p, and a maximum file size of 250 MB; links are accepted only for public videos from TikTok, Facebook, X, or Reddit. Longer, larger, or higher-resolution videos must be trimmed, compressed, or downscaled before upload, and shorter or lower-resolution videos cannot be checked. This limitation is set to keep upload handling, preprocessing, and inference within a manageable range for a research prototype and to prevent batch-style or high-throughput use that falls outside the intended scope of the study.
22. Respondent sample. End users were drawn from the University of the East community, aged 15 to 59, through quota and purposive sampling, and experts from organizations in Metro Manila through purposive sampling. Because these are non-probability samples, the results describe the participating respondents and cannot be generalized to all social-media users. The sample size was set with Cochran’s formula, which assumes random sampling; with quota and purposive sampling, it served as a planning target, and no sampling margin of error is claimed for the results.
23. Self-reported ratings. The ISO/IEC 25010-based questionnaires record how respondents perceived the platform during short, first-time, task-based sessions with researcher-provided or authorized test clips; during the evaluation period, the built-in sample clips were all deepfakes. Security is rated as perceived and is not verified through penetration testing or a formal security audit; uploads pass through third-party hosting and GPU services, and their automatic deletion is not independently audited. Performance-efficiency ratings reflect the server’s speed and availability between 12 and 30 October 2026, including start-up delays after idle periods.
