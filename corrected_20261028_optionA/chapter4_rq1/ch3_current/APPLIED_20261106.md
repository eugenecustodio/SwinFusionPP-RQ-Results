## Likert rating interpretation added to Statistical Treatment (same day, night), SUGGEST

**Inserted after the expert thematic-synthesis paragraph:** two paragraphs.
- The weighted-mean definition, WM = Σ(f×x)/N (Pimentel, 2019, p. 186).
- Mean and SD for composite Likert scores (Joshi et al., 2015, pp. 401–402; Norman, 2010, as summarized in Pimentel 2019, p. 184).
- Equal-width intervals from Pimentel (2019, Table 5, p. 188):
  - 4.20–5.00 = Excellent / Strongly Agree;
  - 3.40–4.19 = Good / Agree;
  - 2.60–3.39 = Acceptable / Neutral;
  - 1.80–2.59 = Poor / Disagree;
  - 1.00–1.79 = Very Poor / Strongly Disagree.
  - The labels come from each instrument's scale: Appendix C for end users, Appendix D for experts. Pimentel p. 188 says the labels can be adapted.
- Means are rounded to 2 decimals before interpretation, which closes the 0.01 gaps between intervals.

**References added (plain text; italics by hand):**
- Joshi et al. (2015), DOI 10.9734/BJAST/2015/14975;
- Norman (2010), DOI 10.1007/s10459-010-9222-y;
- Pimentel (2019), IJSBAR 45(1), 183–191.

**Sources:** the PDFs are saved in ./sources/. Lindner & Lindner (2024) uses rounding-based "true limits" (4.51–5.00 …). It was not used, because Pimentel (2019, pp. 187–189) shows that convention concentrates means in the middle category.

**Correction:** the 4.21–5.00 / 1.81–2.60 bands suggested earlier in chat were not verifiable in any source, so they were replaced by Pimentel's verified bands.
