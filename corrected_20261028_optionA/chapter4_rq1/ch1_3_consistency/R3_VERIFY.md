**C15** → [NEW] Deepfake harms are not evenly distributed. In many real-world contexts, the individuals most vulnerable to deception are not necessarily those most capable of verifying media authenticity. Because SwinFusion++ is developed as both a detection model evaluated under cross-dataset shift and a deployable platform evaluated for usability and software quality, its significance is inherently societal, technical, and institutional—bridging detection performance with real-world adoptability.

**C16** → [NEW] Older Generations and Not Digitally Literate Users. Older generations and individuals with limited digital literacy are disproportionately exposed to manipulation risks because they are more likely to treat video as persuasive evidence and less likely to have the tools or technical familiarity needed for verification. By developing a detection system that can be deployed through an accessible web platform and designed to communicate outputs clearly (screening label, model score or calibrated estimated probability, and metadata), this study contributes toward stronger safeguards against malicious deepfake use–reducing risks of deception, misinformation exposure, identity theft, harassment, and reputational harm.
