                        Swin Transformer for Deepfake Detection

Abstract– Combining spatial and temporal representations does not establish that cross-attention improves deepfake detection. This
study evaluates SwinFusion++, a face-aligned dual-stream detector integrating Swin-B and Video Swin-B through tubelet-aligned, lo-
cal, bidirectional, gated hierarchical cross-attention (TLBG-HCA). Evaluation covers FaceForensics++ DeepFakes C23/C40, Celeb-DF
v2, and an audited DeeperForensics-1.0 binary construction. Video-level comparisons distinguish documented training cohorts, indi-
vidual models, homogeneous seed ensembles, and a single-seed frozen-backbone fusion experiment. Uncertainty analysis accounts for
recorded dependency groups; frozen transfer retains source-trained weights, calibration, and thresholds. Fusion ensembles achieved
ROC-AUCs of 0.9965, 0.9854, 0.9724, and 0.9990 across the four respective conditions, without consistent superiority over stan-
dalone encoders. Against the representative CNN–LSTM on C40, seed-1701 fusion improved AUC by 0.1846 (95% paired interval
0.1503–0.2165; Holm-adjusted p = 0.0003999). However, full TLBG-HCA and the capacity-matched no-exchange head had identical
observed AUC and F1 in the controlled experiment; no incremental exchange advantage was established. Frozen transfer to the full
DeeperForensics construction yielded AUC 0.8021 but fake recall only 13.41%; strict lineage filtering yielded AUC 0.7682 and recall
17.90%. Calibration limitations and additional computational cost further qualify practical interpretation. These findings support bench-
mark discrimination more strongly than exchange-specific benefit or reliable transfer, demonstrating why architectural performance,
mechanism attribution, and operating-point validity require separate evidence.
